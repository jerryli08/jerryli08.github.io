FRC REBUILT Robot

Team 2856 Planetary Drive

Designed solo in my first FRC season. It played in the playoffs at the Smoky Mountains Regional.

I did all of the CAD for this robot for FRC team 2856, Planetary Drive, from Paul Laurence Dunbar High School in Lexington, Kentucky. The team had never used CAD before. I had a friend on the team, so I did it for them. It was my first FRC season.

The robot has a pivoting roller intake, a hopper that splits fuel into three lanes, a two-shaft transfer and three shooters, on a swerve drive. At the 2026 Smoky Mountains Regional the team ranked 21st of 46, was picked onto the 7th alliance and went out in the second round of the playoffs.

Loading 3D

  1. Fuel on the floor

    REBUILT is played with foam balls called fuel. Robots pick it up off the floor and shoot it into their alliance's hub. The fuel is on the left, the robot on the right.

  2. Swerve drive

    Four REV MAXSwerve modules, one in each corner of a 26.5 in square frame. Each wheel steers on its own, so the robot can drive in any direction without turning first. Here it drives the way its wheels point in the CAD.

  3. Intake

    A full-width roller at the front pulls fuel in: a 22.6 in polycarbonate tube with a silicone sleeve on a 1/2 in hex axle. A NEO 2.0 on the frame spins it through two belts, 12 to 24 teeth up to a pulley on the pivot axis, then 24 to 24 teeth out to the roller. The side plates are hidden here so both belts show.

  4. Intake pivot

    The whole arm swings about one axis. A NEO on a 25:1 MAXPlanetary drives a 12 tooth sprocket, and #25 chain runs up to a 40 tooth sprocket on the pivot: 83.3:1 in total, counted from the CAD. A REV Through Bore Encoder sits beside the pivot. The CAD is modelled with the arm down; the swing here only shows the axis.

  5. Into the hopper, toward the hub

    Fuel collects in the hopper. Its floor peaks at the intake pivot and slopes down toward the back, and two divider plates split it into three lanes, one per shooter.

  6. Transfer

    A section through the middle lane. At the bottom of each lane two stacked sets of 1.625 in flex wheels, four low and three high, lift the fuel up a curved polycarbonate backing. Each set is one 1/2 in hex shaft across the whole robot; a belt ties the two shafts 1:1 and a NEO 2.0 sits at each end.

  7. Three shooters

    Each lane ends in its own shooter: a Kraken X60 and two 4 in wheels that pinch the fuel against a 3D printed hood. The hood curls forward at the top, so the fuel leaves up and forward, over the robot. The arcs are drawn, not measured.

  8. The whole robot

    In the CAD the frame is 26.5 in square, 33.0 in over the bumpers, the wheelbase is 23.0 in square and the robot stands 22.0 in tall. It has 15 motors: 8 in the swerve modules, 3 Kraken X60s on the shooters, 3 NEO 2.0s on the roller and the transfer, and 1 NEO on the pivot.

The job

Planetary Drive had never used CAD. I did all of the robot's CAD in Fusion 360, and the team built the robot from the model. It was my first FRC season.

The model is mostly off-the-shelf FRC parts, REV MAXSwerve and MAXPlanetary, AndyMark pre-drilled tube and angle, ThriftyBot roller parts and WCP flex wheels, plus custom plates: aluminum shooter plates and belly pan, polycarbonate intake and hopper plates, and 3D printed spacers, mounts and the three hoods.

Frame and drivetrain

The intake is at the front, the shooters are at the back, and everything between them is hopper. Under it is a 26.5 in square frame of 2x1 in pre-drilled aluminum box tube, 19.5 in rails between the four MAXSwerve modules, joined by gussets, with a triangle-pocketed aluminum belly pan. The roboRIO, the power distribution panel and the battery sit on the pan.

The modules use 3 in wheels and the medium speed gearing, each with a NEO to drive and a NEO 550 to steer. They were first wired up on a wooden test frame; the aluminum frame and pan show up in the build photos a week later.

Why the roller keeps running at any arm angle

The NEO 2.0 that spins the roller sits on the frame, not on the arm. Its belt climbs 12 to 24 teeth to a pulley on the pivot axis, and a second belt runs 24 to 24 teeth from there out to the roller, 2:1 overall. With the middle pulley on the pivot axis, both belts keep fixed centres wherever the arm is: the first because the motor and the pivot are both on the frame, the second because the pivot and the roller are both on the arm.

Swing the arm and watch the two distances. The arm is a pair of 3/8 in polycarbonate plates, 12.0 in from pivot to roller in the CAD.

Loading 3D

Side view from the roller motor side, outer plates hidden. The slider turns the arm about its real pivot axis from the CAD; its range is only a demonstration, not the robot's travel.

Hopper and transfer

The hopper walls are 0.177 in polycarbonate. Over the intake there is a front extension of side panels, a front wall and a hinged top panel, and in the CAD it is spaced off the arm plates. The hopper floor peaks at the intake pivot and slopes down toward the back, and two divider plates split it into three lanes.

At the back of each lane the fuel meets two stacked sets of 1.625 in, 60A flex wheels, four low and three high. They push it up a bent polycarbonate backing that curves from the floor up into the shooter. The lower and upper sets each sit on one 1/2 in hex shaft that runs the full width of the robot; an HTD belt on 92 mm centres links them 1:1, and a NEO 2.0 sits at each end.

Two parts in the model carry WestCoast Products' names from their open 2026 Competitive Concept robot: the bent polycarbonate shooter backing (WCP-CC2026-05-009) and the lane divider plates.

Three shooters

Three shooters sit side by side, one per lane. Each is a Kraken X60 and a short 1/2 in hex shaft carrying two 4 in wheels, with a 3D printed hood behind them. The wheels pinch the fuel against the hood, and the hood curls forward at the top, so the fuel leaves up and forward over the robot.

The shooter side plates are aluminum with triangular pockets and the team name cut through them. The Feb 11 model still had solid plates with a few holes; by Feb 13 the cut plates had the pockets and the lettering.

At the Smoky Mountains Regional

The team ranked 21st of 46 in qualifications and was picked onto the 7th alliance. The alliance went out in the second round of the playoffs, in a lower bracket match the 6th alliance won 134 to 110. Early in that match the broadcast commentator calls Planetary Drive stuck on the bump.
